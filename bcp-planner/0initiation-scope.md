# Phase 0: Initiation & Scope

This phase sets the boundaries and authority for everything downstream. Skipping it leads to a BIA that either tries to cover the entire organization at once (never finishes) or quietly excludes something critical because nobody decided it was in scope.

## Sponsorship & Ownership

* Name an executive sponsor — someone with the authority to compel process owners across business units to participate in BIA interviews and to fund recovery strategies later
* Name a BCP program owner — the person actually running this process day to day, distinct from the sponsor
* Without both, later phases stall: interviews get skipped ("too busy"), and Phase 2 strategy decisions have no budget authority behind them

## Scope Definition

Ask directly rather than assuming:

* Which business units are in scope for this cycle? Which are explicitly out (and why — e.g., already covered in a prior cycle, genuinely low-risk, deferred)?
* Which physical sites/locations are in scope?
* Is this a full-program BIA (every process, every unit) or a targeted one (e.g., only customer-facing revenue processes, or only processes affected by an upcoming system migration)? Targeted scope is legitimate and often more realistic than "everything" for a first cycle.

## BCP Policy

Document, even briefly:

* Program objectives — what the organization is trying to achieve (e.g., "resume all Tier 1 processes within RTO after any single-site loss")
* Governance — who approves the BIA findings, who approves recovery strategy spend, who owns plan maintenance
* Review cadence — how often the whole program is revisited (commonly annual, plus trigger-based reviews — see Phase 4)

## Regulatory & Contractual Obligations

* Does any regulation require a documented BCP/DR capability for this industry (financial services, healthcare, critical infrastructure — varies by jurisdiction)?
* Do any customer contracts include continuity or recovery-time commitments that must be reflected in internal RTO targets?
* Flag these obligations now — they often set a floor under RTO/RPO targets that Phase 1 negotiates around, not something to discover for the first time in Phase 1.

## Stakeholder / Interview List

* Build a named list of process owners per in-scope business unit before Phase 1 interviews begin
* For each: name, role, process(es) they own, and whether they're the right person to answer BIA questions (sometimes the nominal owner delegates operational knowledge to someone else — confirm who to actually interview)

## Deliverables (Exit Criteria for Phase 0)

- [ ] Executive sponsor and BCP program owner named
- [ ] Scope defined (business units, sites, processes in/out)
- [ ] BCP policy documented (objectives, governance, review cadence)
- [ ] Regulatory/contractual continuity obligations identified
- [ ] Stakeholder/interview list built with named process owners
