# ISO/IEC 27001 — Information Security Management System (ISMS)

ISO 27001 certifies an organization's **Information Security Management System**, not a single product or codebase. The unit of compliance is the management system and its controls (Annex A), assessed through external certification audits — not something this skill (or any self-assessment) can grant on its own.

## Scope Definition

* ISMS scope statement — which business units, systems, locations, and data are in/out of scope. A narrower stated scope is legitimate and common, but it must be explicit and defensible, not vague.
* Interested parties and their requirements identified (customers, regulators, contracts) — these often drive what "must" be in scope.

## Risk Assessment & Treatment

* Risk assessment methodology defined and consistently applied to the assets/information in scope
* Risk treatment plan — for each identified risk: accept, mitigate, transfer, or avoid — with a named owner
* **Statement of Applicability (SoA)** — for every Annex A control, state whether it's applicable to this ISMS scope and why, and which implemented control satisfies it. This is the document auditors anchor the entire audit to.

## Annex A Control Domains (ISO/IEC 27001:2022)

| Domain | Controls | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| A.5 Organizational | 37 | Policies, roles & responsibilities, asset management, supplier relationships, incident management, business continuity, legal/regulatory compliance |
| A.6 People | 8 | Screening, terms of employment, security awareness/training, disciplinary process, remote working, confidentiality agreements |
| A.7 Physical | 14 | Physical security perimeters, equipment security, clear desk/screen, secure disposal |
| A.8 Technological | 34 | Access control, cryptography, logging & monitoring, secure development, vulnerability management, backup, network security |

Map the specific ask to one or more of these domains — don't run all 93 controls against every request.

## Evidence Typically Required

* Documented ISMS policy set
* Risk register and treatment plan
* Statement of Applicability, kept current as systems change
* Internal audit reports and management review minutes
* Security awareness training records
* Incident log
* Supplier due-diligence records

## Certification Path

* **Stage 1 audit** — documentation review: is the ISMS designed and documented adequately
* **Stage 2 audit** — implementation review: is it actually operating (evidence sampling, staff interviews), not just documented
* **Surveillance audits** — annual, covering a subset of controls
* **Recertification** — full audit every 3 years

## Common Pitfalls

* SoA written once for the initial audit and never updated as systems and risks change
* Risk register created for the audit and never revisited between cycles
* Controls documented on paper but not observably operating in practice — auditors sample live evidence, not policy text alone

## Guidance for This Skill When ISO 27001 Is In Scope

* Map the ask to the specific Annex A domain(s) involved, not "security" in the abstract
* Ask what's driving the request — initial certification, surveillance-audit readiness, or a customer security questionnaire — evidence depth differs sharply between these
* Never state or imply "ISO 27001 compliant" or "certified" — only an accredited certification body can certify; this skill can only assess control readiness against the Annex A framework
* Recommend a qualified internal auditor or the external certification body as the actual sign-off authority before any certification claim is made

## Deliverables Checklist

- [ ] ISMS scope statement defined and justified
- [ ] Risk assessment methodology applied and risk register maintained
- [ ] Risk treatment plan with named owners per risk
- [ ] Statement of Applicability covers all Annex A controls with applicability justification
- [ ] Evidence identified/mapped per applicable control
- [ ] Internal audit and management review cadence established
